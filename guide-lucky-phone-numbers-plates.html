The economics of lucky numbers

By the 111158 Editorial Team · Updated September 2026

Quick answer In Chinese-speaking markets, lucky numbers are a status asset. Hong Kong's government plate auctions have fetched millions: plate "28" (易发, "easy prosperity") sold for about US$2.3M in 2016. A Chengdu phone number made of eight 8s was bought by an airline for roughly US$280,000. Academic research also finds measurable price premiums for lucky digits.

Why numbers become assets

  • Scarcity: only a few short, 8-heavy combinations exist.
  • Visibility: plates and phone numbers are shown publicly every day.
  • Signalling: they broadcast success, taste and wealth.
  • Charity: many plate auctions fund public causes, which adds prestige.

What brands can learn

If customers pay premiums for lucky numbers, your numbers carry meaning too: your hotline, your address, your prices and your launch date. Getting them right costs little and removes friction. Getting them wrong (a 4-heavy hotline, say) quietly costs trust.
